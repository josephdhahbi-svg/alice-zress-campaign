# Mystryl — Canon-Guarded Moonveil Integration

Status: **MAJOR COSMOLOGY BRAINSTORM / SOURCE VERIFICATION REQUIRED**
Purpose: preserve the user's Mystryl concept while separating Forgotten Realms history from Moonveil-original mythology.

## Core role

Mystryl is elevated as one of the central historical anchors of the Moonveil saga, especially for Netheril, the Weave, Karsus's Folly, Mystra succession, the Starweave Necromancer and later questions about magical freedom versus control.

She is important, but Moonveil does **not** automatically make her the literal creator of every magical system, road, artifact, scroll, planar gate or creature in the multiverse.

## Canon-facing foundation to verify against source library

High-confidence Forgotten Realms concepts to preserve:
- Mystryl is an earlier goddess of magic and predecessor in the Mystra succession.
- She is intimately associated with the Weave and Netheril's age of extraordinary arcane magic.
- Karsus cast Karsus's Avatar during Netheril's catastrophe.
- The event caused a catastrophic disruption of magic.
- Mystryl sacrificed herself; a later incarnation/successor of the goddess of magic restored/restructured magic.
- Netheril's flying enclaves and mythallars belong to this historical arc.

Exact dates, wording, divine mechanics, spell restrictions, city histories and succession details must be source-checked before canonization.

## Claims retained only as Moonveil hypotheses

The following are **NOT treated as established Forgotten Realms canon**:
- Mystryl awakened when the first stars formed.
- She created the first spell.
- She created the first magical creature.
- She created the first planar gate.
- She personally taught Netherese people how to build every mythallar.
- every Netherese city had a Mystryl shrine.
- she created the first treasure-marking language.
- her death fractured one Arcane Road into the Twelve Roads.
- her death created the Patchwork Architect.
- multiverse fractures are literally her heartbeat.
- Uvaeren is a Netherese city.
- the listed Mystryl artifacts/scrolls/markings are official published relics.

These remain excellent **Moonveil mystery propositions** that characters may believe, research, disprove or partially confirm.

# The Three Layers of Mystryl

## Layer I — HISTORICAL MYSTRYL
The source-grounded goddess of magic in ancient Netheril's history.

## Layer II — REMEMBERED MYSTRYL
What later cultures believe about her.

Different traditions can contradict each other:
Netherese survivor accounts;
Mystra's churches;
archmage scholarship;
Eilistraeean oral tradition;
Shadowfell records;
Redactor-altered archives;
Astraeum engineering records;
Frankenstein's scientific interpretation.

This lets myths exist without falsely becoming canon facts.

## Layer III — MOONVEIL MYSTRYL
The campaign's unresolved cosmological possibility:
perhaps traces of Mystryl's final magical event propagated through systems later called Roads.

Whether those traces are divine remnants, Weave scars, copied engineering, myth, or something stranger remains discoverable gameplay.

Frankenstein can discover that ancient scholars used twelve-fold diagrams resembling her model.

That creates the question:
**Did Frankenstein rediscover an ancient system—or impose a modern pattern on unrelated evidence?**

Then the Thirteenth Road of Choice becomes even more significant because it does not fit the alleged Mystryl model.

# Mystryl and Frankenstein

Frankenstein initially interprets Mystryl as the ultimate example of a creator/engineer sacrificing herself to preserve her creation.

First Daughter challenges this:
**“Was the Weave hers because she made it—or was she responsible for it because others depended upon it?”**

This advances Frankenstein's central philosophy:
Creation does not grant ownership.
Preservation does not grant ownership.
Power creates responsibility, not possession.

Laboratory Fourteen may contain a silver-tear diagram thousands of years older than Frankenstein's Stitched Eye. This is evidence of similarity, not proof of causation.

# Mystryl and the Starweave Necromancer

This should be one of the strongest character connections.

The Mystra-aligned Starweave Necromancer studies the succession of magical divinity as a warning against treating the Weave as merely a weapon.

Her arc can ask:
- Can necromancy preserve without possessing?
- Is undeath an interruption of natural history or another form of magical continuity?
- What does being Chosen mean if divine offices themselves change?
- Should an archlich protect magical knowledge that mortals may misuse?

Mystryl becomes historical mirror rather than a free power-up.

# 30-book progression

**Books 1–5 — RUMOR**
Silver-tear iconography and contradictory fragments. Nobody yet knows whether they are actually Mystryl-related.

**Books 6–10 — HISTORY**
Scholars compare markings, Netherese records and Mystra traditions. Twelve-Road hypothesis appears.

**Books 11–15 — ENGINEERING**
Mythallar/Fate Engine research reveals that later Netherese engineers may have attempted to reproduce patterns attributed to Mystryl.

**Books 16–20 — ECHOES**
Characters encounter magical scars/echoes. “Echo” does not automatically mean Mystryl's conscious spirit.

**Books 21–27 — COSMOLOGY**
Far Realm, Fate and Patchwork evidence challenge the assumption that all strange magic originates in the Weave.

**Books 28–30 — TRUTH**
The party finally has enough evidence to adjudicate the competing theories.

# Book 30 — three hypotheses, not one predetermined reveal

## HYPOTHESIS A — THE SACRIFICE ECHO
Mystryl's death produced a magical echo later exploited by the Patchwork Architect.

## HYPOTHESIS B — THE FALSE MOTHER
The Architect deliberately attributed its system to Mystryl because mortals trust ancient divine explanations.

## HYPOTHESIS C — CONVERGENT DISCOVERY
Neither created the other. Both discovered the same deeper mathematical/cosmic structure.

Additional hypotheses can survive until play establishes evidence.

# Possible endgame choices

If evidence supports a Mystryl-related crisis, the heroes might:
- restore a lost Weave function without resurrecting Mystryl;
- attempt divine restoration only if cosmology/source rules permit it;
- preserve Mystra's current order;
- distribute stewardship among mortals/divinities;
- seal the dangerous system;
- establish a free Road stewardship compact;
- refuse the false binary and seek another solution.

No ending automatically makes a PC “the new Weavekeeper” without enormous prerequisites and consequences.

# Central question

Mystryl's Moonveil story ultimately asks:

**Is magic something a god owns, something mortals control, or a relationship everyone who uses it is responsible for?**

This ties Mystryl to the saga's larger principle:

**Power without domination. Guidance without predestination. Freedom with responsibility.**
