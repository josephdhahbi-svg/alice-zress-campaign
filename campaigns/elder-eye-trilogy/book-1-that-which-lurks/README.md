# Book I — That Which Lurks

## Function
**Discovery.** Book I turns rumors into proof. It establishes the living world around Eilistraee, Lolth and the hidden Elder Eye without explaining the mystery too early.

## Seven thematic pillars
1. Sword — courage and defense.
2. Song — memory and communication.
3. Dance — movement, identity and ritual.
4. Moonlight — truth and guidance.
5. Mercy — strength expressed through restraint.
6. Freedom — the right to choose one's path.
7. Hope — the outcome the heroic side tries to create for others.

## Book-I design promise
The setting must feel alive before the crisis begins. Every chapter should advance multiple systems: people, culture, politics, religion, environment, economy, relationships and the mystery.

## Five-act working frame
**Act I — The Living Moon:** establish ordinary life, culture, relationships and competing pressures.

**Act II — Things Missing:** disappearances, altered routes, strange residues, rumors and political exploitation.

**Act III — The Wrong Darkness:** evidence stops fitting the expected Lolth/Eilistraee conflict; Ghaunadaur/Elder Eye traces strengthen.

**Act IV — Beneath the Song:** investigation crosses into forbidden geography, hidden cult activity and dangerous history.

**Act V — Proof:** the protagonists obtain undeniable evidence that the Elder Eye threat is real. The victory is knowledge/survival, not final defeat.

## Required ending outputs
Book I must hand Book II:
- verified route or clue toward the Labyrinth;
- identified surviving witnesses/allies;
- at least one Elder Eye relic/evidence record;
- unresolved Lolthite political consequences;
- Eilistraeean cultural record/song/ritual carrying memory forward;
- explicit Hope consequence showing whom the heroes saved or empowered;
- continuity packet of dates, locations, sources and unresolved contradictions.

## Player-facing scene rule
When a scene becomes playable, provide five meaningful choices. At least one should permit a compassionate/hopeful solution when logically available, but no choice is guaranteed success.
