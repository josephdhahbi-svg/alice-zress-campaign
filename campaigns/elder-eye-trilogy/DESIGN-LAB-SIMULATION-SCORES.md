# Elder Eye Design Lab — Simulation & Improvement Scores

**Purpose:** turn brainstorming into repeatable design experiments instead of adding disconnected lore.

## Evaluation model

Every candidate scene/system is scored 0–10 on ten axes:
1. Fun / player desire to continue
2. Agency / five choices matter
3. Living-world depth
4. Elder Eye mystery strength
5. Seven-theme integration
6. Character/relationship consequence
7. Continuity safety
8. World-building coverage
9. Replayability / divergent state
10. Future callback value

**Weighted score /100**
- Fun 15%
- Agency 15%
- Living world 12%
- Mystery 12%
- Themes 8%
- Relationships 8%
- Continuity 10%
- World-building 8%
- Replayability 6%
- Callback 6%

### Gates
- Any Continuity score < 7: QUARANTINE.
- Any Agency score < 6: rewrite choices.
- Any Fun score < 7: redesign before production.
- Final candidate target: >= 82/100.
- Strong production target: >= 90/100.

## Simulation assumptions

These are design simulations, not claims of real-player testing. Each pass models five player styles:
- Completionist Hero
- Lore Investigator
- Tactical Optimizer
- Social/Diplomatic Player
- Chaos/Exploration Player

A scene passes only if at least four of five styles receive a meaningful decision, discovery, relationship change, resource change, or world-state consequence.

## Experiment 01 — Book I opening: The Living Moon

**Prototype:** an Eilistraeean community night scene begins as ordinary life—trade, food, song, work, patrols and relationships—before a missing-person pattern interrupts the celebration.

Initial scores:
Fun 8; Agency 6; Living World 9; Mystery 8; Themes 9; Relationships 7; Continuity 7; World Building 9; Replayability 6; Callback 8.
**Weighted result: 77.9/100 — REVISE.**

Failure found: five choices could become five flavors of investigation.

### Iteration A
Differentiate the five choices by system:
1. SWORD — join the perimeter search; security clue + patrol relationship.
2. SONG — preserve/perform a changed verse; memory clue + cultural relationship.
3. DANCE — follow ritual footwork to a physically hidden route; navigation clue.
4. MERCY — protect/interview a frightened suspect; social clue + faction trust.
5. FREEDOM/HOPE — organize civilians rather than chase the threat; community resilience + later survival modifier.

Re-score:
Fun 9; Agency 9; Living World 9; Mystery 9; Themes 10; Relationships 9; Continuity 8; World Building 9; Replayability 9; Callback 10.
**Weighted result: 90.2/100 — PRODUCTION CANDIDATE.**

## Experiment 02 — Elder Eye clue pressure

Three clue densities modeled across five acts:
- Low: 1 major clue/act — mystery survives but risks feeling stalled.
- Medium: 2 major clues/act — best balance.
- High: 4+ major clues/act — reveals Ghaunadaur too early.

**Selected model:** 2 major clues per act, 10 major clues total, with minor environmental echoes allowed. Major clue ladder:
Absence -> residue -> repeated pattern -> false explanation challenged -> name fragment -> cult link -> route geometry -> historical contradiction -> surviving witness/relic -> proof.

Do not require all ten in one playthrough. Five-choice branches should expose different combinations while preserving enough redundancy to avoid a dead-end investigation.

## Experiment 03 — Living-world pressure

A scene containing only quest NPC + clue + combat scored poorly for world vitality. Require at least **3 ordinary-life signals** before or during disruption:
- livelihood/work/trade,
- relationship/custom/recreation,
- authority/service/environment.

This makes the Elder Eye corruption measurable because the player sees what normal life looked like before it changes.

## Experiment 04 — Imported Omni-Engine stress test

Test question: does V106–V112 material strengthen Book I if activated immediately?

Result: **NO for direct activation; YES as indexed future infrastructure.**

Reasons:
- Celestial, infernal and convergence engines expand scope faster than Book I needs.
- Far Realm concepts can strengthen the mystery, but named crossover content can contaminate continuity.
- Cumulative migration archives create code risk until compared with the live DB helper.

Decision: extract structures, not claims. Book I gets anomaly/madness/route concepts only when they support a scene already required by the roadmap.

## Experiment 05 — 225-cell matrix practicality

Filling all 225 cells to equal depth before writing scenes causes planning drag.

**Improved model: progressive resolution.**
- Tier A: 15 station summaries.
- Tier B: active-story cells only.
- Tier C: cells touched by a scene/choice.
- Tier D: full-detail cells when a route becomes playable.

Book I priority cells: Material Plane, Elder Eye Depths, Far Realm Boundary, Infinite Doors foreshadowing; plus the pillars Culture, Politics, Factions, Economy, Environment, Daily Life, Conflict, Characters, Theme, Navigation.

## Next simulation loop

1. Build Book I Act 1 as five playable scenes.
2. Score each scene with the 100-point model.
3. Rewrite every scene below 82.
4. Simulate five player styles.
5. Track which clues, relationships, factions and world states each route exposes.
6. Test whether Act 1 can reach Act 2 without a mandatory single choice.
7. Repeat for Acts 2–5.
8. Only then convert approved records into game/database implementation.

## Improvement principle

**Brainstorm -> model -> score -> simulate -> find failure -> revise -> re-score -> promote or reject.**

New ideas are not canon merely because they are imaginative. Promotion requires story value, continuity classification, causal connection, and a future callback.
