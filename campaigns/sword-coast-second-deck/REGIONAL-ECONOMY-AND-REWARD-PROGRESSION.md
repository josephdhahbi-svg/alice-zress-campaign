# MEGA-002 Regional Economy, Government & Reward Progression

Status: **LOCKED ECONOMY FRAMEWORK**
Source baseline: user-provided 2024 PHB/Basic Rules, 2024 DMG, and Baldur's Gate Gazetteer in *Baldur's Gate: Descent into Avernus*. Moonveil additions are labeled CAMPAIGN RULE.

## 1. Published price baseline
Use 2024 equipment/service prices as the reference price before local modifiers.
Lifestyle/day: Wretched 0; Squalid 1 SP; Poor 2 SP; Modest 1 GP; Comfortable 2 GP; Wealthy 4 GP; Aristocratic 10 GP.
Travel baseline includes coach 3 CP/mile between towns, 1 CP/mile in a city, road/gate toll 1 CP, ship passage 1 SP/mile.
Hirelings: skilled 2 GP/day minimum; untrained 2 SP/day; messenger 2 CP/mile.
Spellcasting services: cantrip 30 GP; level 1 50 GP; level 2 200 GP; level 3 300 GP; levels 4–5 2,000 GP; levels 6–8 20,000 GP; level 9 100,000 GP, subject to settlement availability and components.

2024 magic-item reference values:
Common 100 GP; Uncommon 400 GP; Rare 4,000 GP; Very Rare 40,000 GP; Legendary 200,000 GP; Artifact PRICELESS.
Consumables other than spell scrolls use half value; spell scroll value follows the 2024 scroll rule. If a magic item incorporates priced mundane equipment, add the mundane item's cost.

## 2. Published treasure baseline
2024 DMG average hoard benchmarks:
CR 0–4: 500 GP, 1d4-1 magic items.
CR 5–10: 4,400 GP, 1d3 magic items.
CR 11–16: 36,000 GP, 1d4 magic items.
CR 17+: 330,000 GP, 1d6 magic items.
Quest rewards can use the same hoard table keyed to character level.

2024 campaign magic-item distribution benchmark:
Levels 1–4: 6 common, 4 uncommon, 1 rare.
Levels 5–10: 10 common, 17 uncommon, 6 rare, 1 very rare.
Levels 11–16: 3 common, 7 uncommon, 11 rare, 7 very rare, 2 legendary.
Levels 17–20: 5 rare, 11 very rare, 9 legendary.
These are whole-party campaign benchmarks, not a requirement that every settlement sell these items.

## 3. Baldur's Gate — published economic/government model
Government: Parliament of Peers (mainly nearly fifty patriars) recommends law/governance; ultimate authority is the Council of Four (three dukes and a grand duke). The Council controls the Flaming Fist, navy, tax collectors, public utilities, etc. The Watch primarily serves the Upper City; Flaming Fist largely handles the Lower City and has nominal Outer City authority.

Economy: Baldur's Gate is a major commercial hub because north/south Sword Coast trade, the Sea of Swords port, and the Chionthar meet there. Exports include fish, fish glue, and sea salt, but trade itself is the major economic force. Large trade guilds and wholesalers move goods along the coast. The market is exceptionally broad, including rare goods and morally questionable commerce. Professional guilds are prominent; crews can provide mutual protection, loans, and economic pressure.

External trade links include Waterdeep/Neverwinter to the north, rivalry with Amn, Chult/Port Nyanzaru connections, and a rare-book market driven partly by Candlekeep travelers.

### Campaign market zones
These are MEGA-002 game abstractions layered on the published city:
- **BG-ECON-U Upper City:** wealthy buyers, elite services, art/jewelry, expensive specialists; mundane reference price usually x1.10–1.50 when scarcity/prestige applies.
- **BG-ECON-L Lower City:** guild/craft/port market; ordinary legal goods usually x0.90–1.15.
- **BG-ECON-O Outer City:** informal markets, animals, cheap labor, secondhand/smuggled goods; x0.70–1.20 but higher fraud/theft/provenance risk.
- **BG-ECON-P Port/wholesale:** bulk cargo, salt, fish, ship supplies, imports; quantity discounts possible, scarcity varies by shipping.
These are not claims of published fixed multipliers; every applied modifier must be logged.

## 4. Sword Coast route economy — campaign model
Treat each route settlement as an ECON-ZONE rather than assuming all towns have Baldur's Gate inventory.
- Levels 1–4: local/basic economy. Mundane gear, food, lodging, tools, common consumables; magic stock usually Common, occasional Uncommon; Rare generally quest/special-market.
- Levels 5–10: regional trade economy. Better specialists and Uncommon stock; Rare becomes obtainable through important merchants/quests; Very Rare exceptional.
- Levels 11–16: major-hub/high-risk economy. Rare is established high-value stock/reward; Very Rare appears through elite markets/quests; Legendary exceptional and story-gated.
- Levels 17–20: world-shaping economy. Very Rare/Legendary rewards become appropriate; artifacts remain priceless/story objects.

Remote places may have less coin even at high level. High-level value can be relics, gems, trade bars, deeds, favors, rare materials, spell services, or faction assets rather than implausibly huge coin piles.

## 6. Level-to-value progression — campaign rule
Goal: lowest-level areas feel materially modest; higher-level areas expose larger wealth and rarer rewards without breaking 2024 benchmarks.

| Level | Reward band per major dungeon* | Typical magic reward ceiling | Market feel |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 1–2 | 50–250 GP value | Common | neighborhood/local |
| 3–4 | 150–750 GP | Common/Uncommon; Rare exceptional | city/road |
| 5–7 | 500–2,500 GP | Uncommon/Rare | regional |
| 8–10 | 1,500–7,500 GP | Rare; Very Rare exceptional | major trade |
| 11–13 | 5,000–20,000 GP | Rare/Very Rare | elite/high-risk |
| 14–16 | 12,000–50,000 GP | Very Rare; Legendary exceptional | planar/major power |
| 17–18 | 30,000–100,000 GP | Very Rare/Legendary | world-scale |
| 19–20 | 75,000–330,000+ GP | Legendary; Artifact story-only | endgame |

*Band is total economic value potentially discoverable/earnable in that dungeon, not guaranteed liquid GP and not additive to the DMG hoard benchmark without checking campaign totals. It can include coin, gems, art, goods, services, property/favors, and magic items. Final observed rewards must be reconciled against the 2024 DMG tier totals and item-distribution targets.

## 7. Reward composition
Every dungeon gets a budget with:
CASH + TRADE GOODS + GEMS/ART + CONSUMABLES + PERMANENT ITEMS + SERVICES/FAVORS + QUEST ASSETS.
Low-level rewards emphasize cash, supplies, tools, common items and modest valuables.
Mid-level rewards add rare materials, specialist services, uncommon/rare magic, businesses/contracts.
High-level rewards add very rare/legendary items, major gems/art, strongholds/deeds/faction resources, high-level spell services.
Endgame rewards may include priceless relics/artifacts but these do not count as spendable cash.

## 8. Market availability is separate from affordability
A character can possess enough gold but still be unable to buy an item if the settlement cannot supply it.
Every shop record:
SHOP-ID | ECON-ZONE | settlement | owner | government/license | faction/guild | inventory tier | base price | local modifier | stock qty | legality | provenance | restock rule.

## 9. Government/economy consequences
Quest decisions update ECON variables:
trade_route_open, beacon_operational, toll_rate, piracy_risk, guild_trust, Fist/Watch standing, refugee_pressure, food_supply, shipping_capacity, Underdark_route_security, relic_legality.
These variables modify later prices and rewards. Example: repairing a bridge lowers freight cost; handing a route to one faction can raise tolls; exposing smuggling can improve legality but remove cheap black-market supply.

## 11. Highest-value placement
The highest ordinary rewards and market access belong near levels 17–20, but artifacts and unique story relics remain PRICELESS rather than converted into GP. DNG/quest difficulty, narrative importance, settlement capacity, and the 2024 item-distribution benchmark all constrain placement.
