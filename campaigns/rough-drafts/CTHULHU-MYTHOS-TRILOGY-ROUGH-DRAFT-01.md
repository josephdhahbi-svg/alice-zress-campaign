# ROUGH DRAFT TRILOGY 01 — CTHULHU MYTHOS: THE THREE IMPOSSIBLE BOOKS

> **Status:** ROUGH DRAFT / CROSSOVER-INSPIRATION EXPERIMENT.
> This trilogy uses cosmic-horror/Cthulhu-Mythos inspiration filtered through Moonveil. It does not claim Lovecraft entities are official D&D canon. D&D lore, campaign-original material, and crossover inspiration must remain source-tagged during later continuity review.

# THE SMASH

Three Moonveil story-books are smashed into three D&D sourcebook/adventure lenses.

## Book I — THE BOOK THAT DREAMS
**Moonveil age:** ORIGIN -> DAWN  
**D&D lens:** **Van Richten's Guide to Ravenloft**  
**Horror lens:** identity, dreams, memory, intimate dread  
**Primary deity:** **Eilistraee**  
**Navigation:** LIGHT  
**Core question:** *If something can dream your memories, what remains yours?*

## Book II — THE BOOK BETWEEN STARS
**Moonveil age:** DAWN -> NOON -> EVENING  
**D&D lens:** **Spelljammer: Adventures in Space**  
**Horror lens:** distance, observation, alien navigation, being noticed  
**Primary deity:** **Mystra**  
**Navigation:** STARS  
**Core question:** *If the universe can be mapped, who decides where mortals are allowed to go?*

## Book III — THE BOOK BENEATH THE EYE
**Moonveil age:** EVENING -> NIGHT  
**D&D lens:** **The Book of Vile Darkness**  
**Horror lens:** forbidden knowledge, corruption, temptation, certainty  
**Primary deity:** **Ghaunadaur**  
**Navigation:** COLOR  
**Core question:** *What would you surrender to know the truth?*

The three books form one equation:

**DREAM / SELF + MAP / WORLD + FORBIDDEN TRUTH / CHOICE = THE MYTHOS**

And the three deity positions form the central theological conflict:

**EILISTRAEE — freedom and chosen identity**  
**MYSTRA — structure, magic, knowledge, boundaries**  
**GHAUNADAUR — hunger, corruption, alien surrender**

This is not a literal three-deity fusion. It is a three-way dramatic architecture.

# BOOK I — THE BOOK THAT DREAMS
## Ravenloft lens / Eilistraee / LIGHT
**Levels:** 1–7

A book is discovered that cannot be read while awake.

Every night one of the five women dreams one page.

The pages are not text. They are places.

The party gradually realizes the dream-book is constructing a domain from their memories. A childhood kitchen becomes a corridor. A market song becomes weather. An argument becomes a locked door. A dead relative becomes a road sign.

The horror is personal: **something is learning how to build reality out of the party's interior lives.**

## 5 opening mysteries
1. A lamp casts the shadow of a room that does not exist.
2. Seven bells reproduce a conversation Brikket never witnessed.
3. Pella maps a hidden chamber that exists only while she sleeps.
4. Rhava measures atmospheric pressure from an impossible ocean.
5. Naeva wakes singing a melody in her own voice that she has never learned.

## 5 cultures encountered
1. **The Waking Houses** — families sleep in shifts so somebody is always awake to remember reality.
2. **The Candle Census** — civic workers count inhabited homes by maintained lights rather than names.
3. **The Unnamed Dead** — families temporarily withhold names from the deceased because dream-things can use names as addresses.
4. **Moon-Dancers of the Open Road** — Eilistraeean-inspired rescuers who teach that identity is chosen through action rather than dictated by fear.
5. **The Pillow Guild** — mundane craftspeople whose stuffing, herbs, stitching and trade suddenly matter because sleep has become infrastructure.

## 5 bosses
1. **The Borrowed Child** — knows everyone's favorite childhood memory.
2. **The House With Teeth** — architecture assembled from fears of home.
3. **The Sleepless Knight** — protects people by forbidding sleep.
4. **The Choir of Your Own Voice** — copies Naeva and then the whole party.
5. **THE DREAMING BOOK** — not destroyed; confronted as a place/intelligence.

## 5 rewards
1. Ilyra — **Moonwake Lantern**, reveals when a familiar route has been dream-altered.
2. Brikket — **Bell of the Waking Witness**, records consent given while awake.
3. Pella — **Oneiric Survey Slate**, maps relationships between dream spaces, not exact terrain.
4. Rhava — **Pressure Glass**, distinguishes physical atmosphere from dream atmosphere.
5. Naeva — **Refrain of Returning**, helps companions recall one agreed waking memory.

These are tools, not automatic solutions.

## Eilistraee's role
Not a deus ex machina. Her thematic presence is expressed through moonlight, music, voluntary community, movement, and freedom. Exact divine manifestations require later source/continuity review.

## Book-I final choice
The Dreaming Book offers each woman a perfectly safe dream-home.

Leaving means accepting danger, grief and uncertainty.

All five can choose independently.

**Book score target: 96+.**

# BOOK II — THE BOOK BETWEEN STARS
## Spelljammer lens / Mystra / STARS
**Levels:** 8–14

The dream-pages from Book I align into a star chart.

Pella discovers the spaces between the pages matter more than the drawings.

The chart leads into wildspace.

There the party encounters civilizations descended from travelers who followed different interpretations of the same ancient map.

Then the stars begin moving when nobody observes them.

9. A navigational spell producing a destination rather than finding one.
10. The realization that the map is also **a lock**.

## 10 bosses/problems

## 10 rewards
The five Book-I tools evolve and five new communal rewards are earned:

## Mystra's role
Mystra represents the possibility that structure and magical law can make knowledge usable without making it total. The conflict is not “Mystra knows everything.” It is whether a Weave/order can remain a tool rather than becoming a prison. Historical/cosmological claims require source verification.

## Book-II revelation
The star map does not point toward the cosmic horror.

It describes the **shape of the cage around it**.

Someone has been erasing routes because navigation itself weakens the cage.

The party has spent two books unknowingly opening it.

**Book score target: 97+.**

# BOOK III — THE BOOK BENEATH THE EYE
## Book of Vile Darkness lens / Ghaunadaur / COLOR
**Levels:** 15–20

The Fifteenth Key opens a route downward that is not spatially down.

Color becomes navigation.

The party reaches a region where moral and physical corruption are deliberately confused. Every forbidden technique is useful. Every terrible shortcut solves a real problem.

The villain's strongest argument is therefore:

**“It works.”**

Ghaunadaur's Elder Eye imagery becomes the campaign's central hostile divine/eldritch pressure. Do not equate Ghaunadaur with the whole Cthulhu Mythos or sovereign of the Far Realm unless explicitly established as campaign-original.

## The three books are physically connected
The Dreaming Book is **the interior**.
The Star Book/Map is **the boundary**.
The Book Beneath the Eye is **the instruction for opening or rewriting the boundary**.

They were never three unrelated artifacts.

They are three mortal interpretations of one impossible structure.

## Ghaunadaur's temptation
The Elder Eye offers certainty:
- know which friend will betray you;
- know which route survives;
- know which god is truthful;
- know whether the dead remain themselves;
- know what lies beyond the cage.

The cost is not initially sanity.

It is **choice**.

If the answer is perfectly known, followers begin believing no alternative is morally defensible.

That links the Cthulhu Mythos directly to Moonveil's Freedom theme.

# FINAL BOSS — THE ANSWER BENEATH ALL QUESTIONS

Do not make the final enemy merely a giant tentacled hit-point pool.

The final encounter has three simultaneous layers:

### BODY
A colossal Elder-Eye manifestation / avatar-scale horror.

### SYSTEM
The Fifteen-Chamber network is collapsing and rerouting civilizations.

### IDEA
The entity offers correct answers to every unresolved question the campaign has accumulated.

Players can win the BODY and lose the IDEA.

They can destroy the monster and still build a world dependent on certainty.

The strongest victory requires:
- surviving the body;
- decentralizing the system;
- refusing to let perfect information replace moral choice.

The five women's original homebrews become the solution:

**PATH** — Ilyra keeps routes open.  
**AGREEMENT** — Brikket builds voluntary coordination.  
**SPACE** — Pella redesigns the cage without central ownership.  
**CHANGE** — Rhava predicts cascading failures.  
**MEMORY** — Naeva preserves why people chose freedom.

The Level-1 professions defeat a Level-20 cosmic problem because they grew rather than being discarded.

# GAME-LAYER DIRECTIVE — BRING THE MYTHOS TO LIFE

## Reference policy

The project may study cosmic-horror tabletop/video-game design and other RPG implementations for **design principles**: investigation loops, sanity/stress alternatives, clue redundancy, journals, relationship pressure, ritual interfaces, environmental storytelling, mystery pacing, boss telegraphs, inventory affordances, and consequence tracking.

**Baldur's Gate 3 is a quality/reference benchmark, not an asset source.** Learn from its readable party presentation, contextual actions, dialogue-choice clarity, tooltips, inspectability, cinematic-to-gameplay transitions, combat readability, quest journal, character sheets, inventories, maps, and consequence-rich conversations. Do **not** copy its copyrighted art, UI assets, text, icons, music, maps, characters, proprietary code, or exact screen layouts. Moonveil must retain its own visual identity and interaction design.

Likewise, Cthulhu-Mythos games can inspire systems and pacing, but their proprietary code/assets/text are not imported unless separately licensed. Public-domain material, open-source code, and permissively licensed assets may be considered only after license verification and attribution requirements are recorded.

## Moonveil Mythos playable loop

**EXPLORE -> NOTICE -> RECORD -> INTERPRET -> CHOOSE -> RISK -> CONSEQUENCE -> CALLBACK**

Every major mystery should support:
- environmental evidence;
- NPC testimony;
- material/object evidence;
- magical/eldritch evidence;
- contradictory evidence;
- at least two viable clue routes so one failed check does not stop the story.

## Five core HUD instruments

1. **Party Rail** — five women, portrait/state/relationship pressure, quick selection.
2. **Context Wheel** — Talk / Inspect / Use / Help / Special; specialist homebrews add contextual actions.
3. **Mythos Journal** — evidence board separating FACT, TESTIMONY, THEORY, CONTRADICTION, UNKNOWN.
4. **5-10-15 Constellation** — discovered nodes visually connect across books without revealing undiscovered answers.
5. **World-State Lens** — communities, routes, factions, promises, anomalies, and consequences changed by the party.

## Character-specific interface actions

- **Ilyra / PATH:** Waymark, Inspect Route, Establish Refuge.
- **Brikket / AGREEMENT:** Witness, Compare Terms, Trace Obligation.
- **Pella / SPACE:** Survey, Map Missing, Overlay Geometry.
- **Rhava / CHANGE:** Measure, Forecast Hazard, Issue Warning.
- **Naeva / MEMORY:** Recall Form, Compare Tradition, Preserve Refrain.

These actions should expose *options and information*, not automatically solve encounters.

## Cosmic-horror pressure: Lucidity, not a punitive "insanity meter"

Avoid reducing cosmic horror to "see monster -> lose sanity." Prototype three interacting states:

- **Lucidity:** ability to distinguish observation from interpretation.
- **Strain:** short-term cognitive/emotional load from impossible events.
- **Anchors:** relationships, routines, songs, places, beliefs, promises, and practices that help a character remain herself.

High Strain can create unreliable interface cues, conflicting theories, or difficult choices, but never treats mental illness as monsterhood. Anchors are story relationships and practices, not a generic healing potion.

## Dialogue

Five-choice conversations remain the default major-decision grammar:
1. direct/action;
2. investigative/reasoned;
3. empathetic/merciful;
4. cultural/relationship;
5. risky/homebrew/specialist.

Choices can be locked/unlocked by knowledge, relationships, items, skills, prior promises, faction status, or world state. Locked options should explain the requirement when revealing it does not spoil the mystery.

## Combat

Use readable turn-based tactical combat as the benchmark:
- clear initiative;
- movement and range previews;
- inspectable status effects;
- environmental interactions;
- reactions;
- elevation/cover where the ruleset supports them;
- contextual specialist actions;
- boss mechanics learned through prior exploration.

Mythos bosses have **Body + System + Idea**. Reducing HP can defeat Body; puzzles/allies can dismantle System; player choices determine whether Idea survives.

## Visual identity

Moonveil should be recognizably its own game:
- dark charcoal/deep-violet base rather than pure black;
- warm gold/crimson accents;
- moon-silver for safe/heroic navigation;
- impossible violet for Origin anomalies;
- constellation lines for connected evidence;
- plate-and-line information compartments;
- rich portraits and location art;
- five large touch-friendly choice buttons on mobile.

## Technical implementation order

1. Finish reliable offline single-player vertical slice.
2. Evidence/Mythos Journal.
3. Party Rail for five women.
4. Context actions tied to the five homebrews.
5. 5-10-15 puzzle/reward/boss data schema.
6. World-state/faction/community consequences.
7. Tactical encounter prototype.
8. Character/inventory/spell/equipment surfaces.
9. Cinematic presentation layer.
10. Only after those are stable: optional network/LLM services.

Do not let generated V35-V220 archive numbering overwrite the verified live SQLite chain. The live repository currently proceeds from V6; new production migrations must continue from verified code and be tested sequentially.

## Definition of "alive"

A Moonveil location is not production-ready merely because it has a quest. Before promotion it should answer:
- Who lives here?
- What do they eat?
- What work keeps the place functioning?
- What do children learn?
- What do people do for fun?
- What do people argue about?
- Who has power and why?
- What is bought/sold/shared?
- What environmental constraint shapes life?
- What happens when the heroes leave?
- What changes because they came?

The universe is successful when players remember communities and people as strongly as bosses.

# LOCKED SETUP — THE SUN, THE MOONS, AND THE STARS

The five-woman adventuring party is collectively called:

## **THE SUN, THE MOONS, AND THE STARS**

Their divine/cosmic five-point frame is:

- ☀ **Lathander — THE SUN** — dawn, renewal, beginnings.
- ☾ **Selûne — THE MOON** — moon, navigation, night-light.
- ♫☾ **Eilistraee — THE SECOND MOON** — moonlight, song, dance, freedom.
- ✦ **Mystra — THE STARS** — magic, pattern, the Weave.
- ◉ **Ghaunadaur / THE EYE** — NOT a party light. The Eye follows them.

## The trilogy image

**The Sun rises.  
The Moons guide.  
The Stars reveal the road.  
And the Eye is always watching.**

The Eye should initially appear at the edges of the story rather than explain itself: an extra shadow, a circular absence on a map, a wrong star, an eye-like reflection, a repeated symbol, a witness who feels observed, a route that seems to watch travelers back.

Across the trilogy the visual progression is:

**BOOK I — LIGHT:** They first notice the Eye.  
**BOOK II — STARS:** They discover the Eye has been following their route.  
**BOOK III — COLOR:** They learn why the Eye followed *them* and must decide what relationship, if any, the universe will have with it.

Do not make Ghaunadaur an ordinary member of the five-person party or reduce the Eye to a standard quest marker. It is the pursuing eldritch presence that turns the party's celestial identity into the central visual motif of the trilogy.

This setup is now the narrative north star for subsequent rough-draft scripts, comics, maps, puzzles, rewards, bosses, and GUI experiments.
