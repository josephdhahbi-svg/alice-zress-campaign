# MOONVEIL ROUGH-DRAFT STORY LAB — Volume 1

> **Status: ROUGH DRAFTS ONLY.** Nothing in this file overrides locked canon.
> Purpose: create aggressively across peoples, cultures, places, builds, conflicts, relationships, mysteries, and all trilogies; later simulate, score, combine, revise, and promote only the strongest material.

## Lab rules

1. Follow the rabbit hole. A good person leads to a family, a family to a custom, a custom to a neighborhood, a neighborhood to an economy, an economy to politics, politics to conflict, conflict to history, history to a ruin, and the ruin back to a person.
2. Make worlds lived-in before making them endangered.
3. Give every culture internal disagreements. No ancestry is a personality.
4. Heroes, villains, and neutrals need ordinary wants in addition to cosmic goals.
5. Each rough draft should eventually connect to LIGHT -> STARS -> COLOR -> one or more of the 15 world-building pillars.
6. Preserve chronology/geography/source auditing. Unknown dates stay unknown.
7. Five playable choices should solve different problems, not merely phrase the same decision five ways.
8. Imported lore and generated ideas remain rough until continuity review.

# RABBIT HOLE 001 — THE MARKET THAT SINGS BACK

**Best fit:** Elder Eye / Underdark foundation  
**People:** drow, svirfneblin, surface expatriates, goblin traders, myconids  
**Core themes:** Song, Mercy, Freedom, Hope  
**Status:** campaign-original rough draft

Before anyone disappears, the market has to matter.

The Moonmarket is not a row of fantasy shops. It is a weekly social agreement. Merchants hang tiny tuned silver disks above their stalls. A buyer does not begin by asking a price; she taps the disk. The merchant answers with a second note. Regular customers know which families use which intervals. Outsiders think it is music. Locals know it is also reputation, credit, warning, flirtation, mourning, and neighborhood gossip.

### Ilyra Venn — drow lamplighter
Ilyra maintains fungus lamps and reflected-moonlight mirrors along the market road. She is not a priestess and dislikes being treated like one merely because she admires Eilistraee. Her practical religion is that nobody should have to walk home frightened.

**Build seed:** mobile support/scout; Dexterity/Wisdom; mirror tools, climbing kit, light magic if later justified.

Her younger brother wants to leave the Underdark permanently. Ilyra wants freedom for him and is furious that his freedom means losing him. That contradiction makes her useful: she believes in freedom when it hurts.

### Brikket-of-Seven-Bells — goblin broker
Brikket sells information by arranging seven mismatched bells. Customers pay for a pattern rather than a sentence. She has survived competing powers by never lying and never telling the whole truth for free.

**Culture rabbit hole:** goblin traders in this district maintain a portable legal tradition. Contracts are witnessed by objects that make distinctive sounds because signatures are useless in darkness, smoke, panic, and among travelers who use different scripts.

**Build seed:** rogue/expert negotiator; Intelligence/Charisma; appraisal, languages, forgery detection.

### Orri Mossfather — myconid gardener
Orri grows edible shelf-fungus in abandoned masonry. He communicates slowly and regards the frantic pace of humanoid politics as a disease. His colony's great dispute is whether sharing memory spores with outsiders is hospitality or cultural surrender.

That disagreement creates two myconid political schools:
- **Open Cap:** memory becomes safer when shared.
- **Closed Ring:** memories are a people’s homeland and should not be exported.

### The mystery
One morning three market disks answer with notes their owners never tuned. Nobody is missing yet. The wrong notes correspond to three stalls whose workers will disappear over the next several days.

The first horror is therefore not a corpse. It is a community realizing that something learned their language.

# RABBIT HOLE 002 — THE FIVE-MINUTE QUEEN

**Best fit:** Elder Eye / Lolthite political counterpoint  
**People:** drow civic families, refugees, spies, temple factions  
**Core themes:** Freedom vs Control; Mercy vs Security  
**Status:** campaign-original rough draft

A neighborhood with no accepted noble ruler elects a temporary emergency leader whenever the warning chimes sound. The office lasts five minutes.

The Five-Minute Queen can close gates, commandeer supplies, and order evacuations. When the fifth minute ends, every order expires unless ordinary civic councils ratify it. The custom arose because residents feared both monsters outside and permanent tyrants inside.

### Sszara Tlin — reluctant emergency queen
Sszara is a former Lolthite logistics officer who defected years ago. She understands authoritarian systems better than anyone in the district, which is exactly why people distrust giving her authority.

**Build seed:** fighter/mastermind hybrid; battlefield logistics rather than supernatural power.

Her story is not “former villain is secretly perfect.” She is efficient, impatient, and genuinely tempted to keep control when frightened people make bad decisions.

### Cultural conflict
Young residents see the Five-Minute Crown as proof that authority can be temporary and accountable. Older survivors see it as a dangerous game with habits learned from tyrants.

When disappearances begin, someone proposes extending Sszara's five minutes to an hour.

That is a political scene before it is a combat encounter.

# RABBIT HOLE 003 — THE CARTOGRAPHER WHO MAPS ABSENCES

**Best fit:** The Labyrinth of the Elder Eye  
**People:** deep gnomes, drow explorers, dwarven surveyors  
**Core themes:** Knowledge, Hope, the limits of maps  
**Status:** campaign-original rough draft

### Pella Nackledeep — svirfneblin negative-space cartographer
Pella does not draw tunnels. She draws places tunnels should exist but do not.

Mining vibrations, air currents, water seepage, old tax records, burial customs, and fungal growth tell her where empty volume ought to be. Her maps are sheets of black stone scratched with silver lines around blank spaces.

**Build seed:** artificer-like surveyor/expert, but class remains undecided pending ruleset selection.

Pella discovers that several impossible blank spaces form a shape only when the maps are stacked vertically. The shape resembles an eye.

She hates this discovery because it violates her professional philosophy: maps should reduce fear, not manufacture it.

### Society rabbit hole
Surveyors here are socially important because maps determine mining rights, burial rights, water ownership, military routes, and inheritance. A cartographic error can bankrupt a clan or start a feud.

Therefore the campaign can make **geography economically consequential** rather than decorative.

# RABBIT HOLE 004 — THE CHILDREN OF TWO SKIES

**Best fit:** Spelljammer Cosmos  
**People:** mixed-generation spelljamming families  
**Core themes:** Stars, migration, identity, home  
**Status:** campaign-original rough draft

Children born aboard long-haul vessels call planetary people **one-sky folk**. They are accustomed to grandparents from different worlds, holidays calculated on incompatible calendars, and arguments over what “down” means.

### Captain Nemea Brasswake — bronze dragonborn navigator
Nemea inherited three contradictory star charts from three generations. Rather than choosing one, she overlays them. The errors reveal historical changes in routes.

**Build seed:** storm sorcerer/navigation officer or ranger-like navigator; exact class pending roster fit.

### Cultural details
- Weddings exchange coordinates rather than rings: “Wherever these two paths cross, there is home.”
- Funerals preserve a final course heading.
- Children learn emergency hull patching before formal arithmetic.
- Wealth is partly measured in reliable destinations, not coins.
- A famous insult translates roughly to “You navigate with only one star.”

### Conflict
A newly safe route will make one port enormously wealthy and another irrelevant. Exploration therefore creates winners and losers.

# RABBIT HOLE 005 — THE INFERNAL PUBLIC DEFENDER

**Best fit:** Baator / Avernus  
**People:** tieflings, petitioners, mortal mercenaries, devils  
**Core themes:** Mercy, law, redemption, contracts  
**Status:** campaign-original rough draft

### Advocate Kestra Vhal
Kestra is a tiefling lawyer who does not believe infernal contracts can be defeated by courage alone. She studies punctuation.

Her clients are mortals who signed contracts under hunger, fear, misinformation, or coercion. She rarely proves a contract never existed. She proves that the other party failed to perform a clause.

**Build seed:** bard/rogue social specialist; no magical “win argument” button.

### Living-world system
Avernian settlements develop entire service economies around contracts:
- translators,
- memory witnesses,
- signature authenticators,
- escape-route guides,
- debt brokers,
- counterfeit hunters,
- neutral scribes.

The heroic party can therefore liberate people by understanding institutions as well as defeating monsters.

Kestra's greatest case concerns a villain the heroes hate. If the contract was unjust, does justice require defending even someone undeserving of sympathy?

# RABBIT HOLE 006 — THE HEAVENLY DISSENTER

**Best fit:** Mount Celestia / Upper Planes  
**People:** aasimar, celestials, mortal pilgrims  
**Core themes:** Justice vs Mercy; perfection vs growth  
**Status:** campaign-original rough draft

### Tiriel-of-the-Unfinished-Wing
Tiriel is a celestial artisan whose left wing is deliberately never completed in paintings or sculpture. She teaches that depicting oneself as finished is a kind of spiritual dishonesty.

She becomes controversial because some pilgrims want heaven to provide certainty. Tiriel insists virtue without choice is merely architecture.

**Build seed:** celestial mentor/support NPC; not intended as a boss.

### Culture
Pilgrims carry stones upward. At each stage they carve away a piece representing an abandoned certainty. The smallest stones can belong to the people who traveled farthest.

A thriving economy emerges around food, lodging, translation, guide services, copying testimonies, repairing equipment, and sending messages back down the mountain.

Even paradise has work.

# RABBIT HOLE 007 — THE CITY THAT RENTS YESTERDAY

**Best fit:** Netherese / chronology trilogy  
**People:** arcanists, laborers, historians, displaced families  
**Core themes:** Memory, power, inequality  
**Status:** campaign-original rough draft; historical placement pending audit

A magical enclave discovers a way to preserve tiny experiential impressions of a day: the smell of rain, the view from a balcony, the sound of a lost singer. The elite collect them as luxuries. Working families use them to preserve memories of relatives.

Then prices rise.

### Aven Marr — human memory mason
Aven repairs storage crystals but cannot afford to access the recording of his own mother.

**Build seed:** wizard/artificer technician, low combat emphasis.

The central question is not whether memory magic is good or evil. It is who owns the past once memory becomes infrastructure.

This can become a bridge into Netherese arrogance without reducing every Netherese person to an arrogant wizard.

# RABBIT HOLE 008 — THE FAR REALM REFUGEE WHO ISN'T MAD

**Best fit:** Far Realm Entropy / Alice MADNESS contrast  
**People:** planar refugees, aberrant beings, suspicious mortals  
**Core themes:** identity, perception, fear of difference  
**Status:** campaign-original rough draft

### Uu-Among-Us
Uu appears incomprehensible: several shadows, one voice arriving a heartbeat early, eyes that look toward yesterday.

But Uu is calm, polite, and terrified.

The dangerous assumption is that alien appearance equals insanity or evil. Uu understands one tiny region beyond conventional reality and has fled something worse.

Uu cannot explain the threat directly because mortal grammar makes several required spatial relationships impossible to express. The party must build a shared vocabulary through objects, music, diagrams, movement, and trust.

**Playable strength:** communication becomes gameplay.

Alice may understand Uu better as her own relationship with eldritch forces deepens, creating both empathy and fear about what she might become.

# RABBIT HOLE 009 — THE DRAGONBORN WEATHER GUILD

**Best fit:** Material / Spelljammer / draconic routes  
**People:** bronze, blue, brass, copper and mixed dragonborn communities  
**Core themes:** environment, science, tradition, public service  
**Status:** campaign-original rough draft

The guild does not worship storms. It measures them.

### Rhava Coilscale
Rhava is an elderly bronze dragonborn engineer who can predict underground flash floods by listening to changes in stone resonance.

Her apprentices disagree over whether magical prediction should replace generations of embodied weather lore.

Their guild maintains public warning towers. Merchants resent shutdown orders. Miners depend on them. Politicians want favorable forecasts.

A weather service becomes a source of culture, science, economics, and political pressure.

# RABBIT HOLE 010 — THE LAST DOOR HAS A KITCHEN

**Best fit:** Infinite Doors / Convergence  
**People:** everyone  
**Core themes:** all seven, especially Hope  
**Status:** campaign-original rough draft

At the cosmic crossroads there is a door nobody notices because it opens into a kitchen.

### Auntie Rell
Species unknown; perhaps deliberately never resolved.

Rell feeds travelers. Gods are expected to wait their turn.

No weapon may be drawn inside, not because of an omnipotent ward but because thousands of travelers across centuries have collectively enforced the custom.

Every wall is covered in recipes annotated by people from incompatible worlds.

The kitchen gives the Infinite Doors something essential: a reason to exist besides transporting adventurers toward bosses.

A portal network becomes civilization when people begin cooking beside it.
