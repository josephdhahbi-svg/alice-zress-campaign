# MOONVEIL ROUGH-DRAFT STORY LAB — Volume 3
## THE TWENTY, THE TWENTY MONSTERS, AND THE FIVE RIVALS

> **ROUGH DRAFT / SIMULATION POOL.** These are candidates, not locked canon. Levels are progression targets for testing story pressure. Builds use D&D vocabulary as inspiration but are not claims of finalized rules legality. Exact historical placement and source compatibility remain subject to continuity review.

## Experiment

Create twenty people across Moonveil's cultures and cosmology. Give each a starting level, a level-appropriate problem, and a future direction. Then create twenty monsters/threats spanning the same progression. Finally select five of the strongest characters and make them recurring rivals who fight, cooperate, lose, learn, and meet again at higher tiers.

The goal is not twenty superheroes. It is twenty lives worth following.

# THE TWENTY CHARACTERS

## LEVEL 1 — ORDINARY PEOPLE AT THE EDGE OF ADVENTURE

### 1. Ilyra Venn — Drow Lamplighter
**Level:** 1  
**Build direction:** scout/support; rogue/ranger vocabulary  
**Home:** Underdark Moonmarket  
**Color:** BLUE candidate  
**Want:** make every road home safe.  
**Problem:** three lamps extinguish in sequence although their fuel is full.  
**Culture:** lamplighters exchange route songs so a missing worker is noticed before a missing lamp.  
**Level-up test:** Ilyra must leave her assigned street to rescue someone beyond her civic responsibility.  
**Future:** becomes a route captain who maps safe corridors without turning them into controlled borders.

### 2. Dorrin Hearthcoin — Hill Dwarf Baker
**Level:** 1  
**Build direction:** artisan/fighter  
**Home:** trade enclave at an Underdark crossroads  
**Want:** reopen his family's communal oven.  
**Problem:** refugees need bread while merchants hoard grain substitutes.  
**Culture:** his clan records major agreements by baking the agreed proportions into ceremonial loaves.  
**Level-up test:** organize a food convoy rather than win a duel.  
**Future:** quartermaster whose logistics can save armies without glorifying war.

### 3. Siva-of-the-Open-Cap — Myconid Memory Tender
**Level:** 1  
**Build direction:** druid/support vocabulary  
**Home:** fungal terraces  
**Want:** share memory spores with outsiders safely.  
**Problem:** a shared memory contains a place nobody in the circle has visited.  
**Level-up test:** enter the unknown memory herself.  
**Future:** diplomat between radically different modes of consciousness.

### 4. Corren Quickstep — Lightfoot Halfling Courier
**Level:** 1  
**Build direction:** rogue  
**Home:** surface/Underdark postal route  
**Want:** deliver every letter, even across political borders.  
**Problem:** one sealed letter is addressed to a person officially dead.  
**Level-up test:** decide whether delivery matters more than law.  
**Future:** builds the first neutral cross-faction message network.

# FIVE STRONGEST RIVALS — THE RICOCHET FIVE

These five are chosen because their values collide productively. They are not necessarily the five highest combat levels at introduction.

## A. Sszara Tlin — SECURITY
Believes freedom survives only when someone is willing to organize defense.
**Weakness:** emergency authority feels increasingly reasonable every time it works.

## B. Kestra Vhal — LAW
Believes rules can restrain stronger powers if ordinary people understand them.
**Weakness:** can confuse procedural fairness with justice.

## C. Nemea Brasswake — DISCOVERY
Believes routes should remain open and knowledge should circulate.
**Weakness:** underestimates what dangerous things also travel through open routes.

## D. Morcant Vale — DIGNITY
Believes living and undead persons deserve ethical treatment independent of fear.
**Weakness:** intellectual distance can become emotional avoidance.

## E. Yara Zerth-Venn — CONSENT
Believes no cosmic authority should decide connectivity for everyone.
**Weakness:** waiting for legitimate consent can itself become a decision with victims.

# RIVAL FIGHT I — LEVEL-5 EQUIVALENT: THE BRIDGE OF FIVE ANSWERS

For simulation, the higher-level candidates are temporarily scaled to a common Level-5-equivalent encounter package. No permanent canon level is changed.

A planar bridge is collapsing. Five groups need it:
- refugees,
- a defense force,
- healers,
- merchants carrying food,
- surveyors carrying route data.

Only two can cross before the first collapse unless somebody changes the situation.

Sszara takes command.
Kestra challenges her authority.
Nemea tries to improvise another route.
Morcant prioritizes the wounded.
Yara refuses to let one leader decide for everyone.

The argument becomes a controlled duel when the bridge's ancient mechanism interprets conflicting commands as hostile claims.

### Round simulation
**Round 1:** Sszara dominates positioning and protects the control platform.  
**Round 2:** Kestra discovers the bridge recognizes formally declared custodianship and contests Sszara's claim.  
**Round 3:** Nemea leaves the fight to climb beneath the bridge, finding a forgotten maintenance path.  
**Round 4:** Morcant deliberately takes a hit to keep two injured civilians out of the duel.  
**Round 5:** Yara realizes the mechanism accepts distributed authority if five different controls are activated simultaneously.

They can win only by stopping the rivalry long enough to cooperate.

**Winner:** nobody individually. Yara solves the structural problem, Nemea finds the route, Kestra legitimizes shared control, Sszara coordinates timing, Morcant preserves lives.

**Score:** Fun 9.2 / Agency 9.5 / Character conflict 9.7 / Living world 9.3 / Replayability 9.0 / Theme 9.6 -> **94.4/100**

**Promotion lesson:** rivals are strongest when each is partly right.

# RIVAL FIGHT II — LEVEL 9: THE ROAD THAT CHARGES MERCY

Years later, the five meet in Avernus.

The sentient Infernal Milestone offers safe passage for ten thousand refugees if the five champions agree that every act of mercy creates an enforceable debt.

Kestra sees exploitable contract language.
Sszara sees ten thousand immediate lives.
Nemea finds an unsafe alternate route.
Morcant argues mercy ceases to be mercy when repayment is mandatory.
Yara insists the refugees themselves must participate in the choice.

This time they actually fight because delay costs lives.

**Provisional duel result:** Sszara defeats Kestra tactically; Nemea outmaneuvers Sszara; Yara traps Nemea's route; Morcant refuses the victory condition and spends his actions evacuating civilians.

The "loser" Morcant earns the most public trust.

**Score:** **95.0/100.**

# RIVAL FIGHT III — LEVEL 13: THE STARLESS NAVIGATOR

The five rivals discover that their arguments have been studied by the Starless Navigator.

It offers them a horrifying compromise: one perfectly controlled planar transportation system that solves Sszara's security, Kestra's enforceability, Nemea's navigation, Morcant's rescue access, and Yara's coordination problems.

The price is centralized control.

For the first time, all five reject the same answer for different reasons.

Their rivalry graduates into a coalition.

But one of them secretly keeps a fragment of the Navigator's control key.

**Do not decide who yet.** Run later simulations with each candidate holding the key and score the consequences.

# LEVEL-PROGRESSION RULE FOR THE TWENTY

When these rough drafts advance, do not level them merely because a chapter ended. Advancement should follow a demonstrated change:

**L1:** choose danger voluntarily.  
**L2:** accept responsibility beyond self.  
**L3:** establish identity and method.  
**L5:** become locally consequential.  
**L7:** affect a region/faction.  
**L9:** change an institution.  
**L11:** influence planar events.  
**L13:** alter routes, wars, or major power structures.  
**L15:** confront world-scale consequences.  
**L17:** transformation/legendary thresholds become possible when individually earned.  
**L20:** culmination, not automatic divinity.

This keeps levels connected to story and prevents every interesting NPC from becoming the same kind of demigod.
