# MOONVEIL ROUGH-DRAFT STORY LAB — Volume 5
## THE COSMIC DAY PARTY: FIVE BOOKS / FIVE ADVENTURES

> **ROUGH DRAFT.** One book for each age of the Cosmic Day. The party begins small. New companions, allies, rivals, cultures, monsters, and responsibilities accumulate naturally as the journey progresses. Nothing here overrides locked trilogy canon.

# Core party at Book I

Begin with five from the Twenty so the group has ideological friction from session one:

1. **Ilyra Venn** — drow lamplighter/scout — safety without domination.
2. **Brikket-of-Seven-Bells** — goblin broker/rogue — information, trade, law.
3. **Pella Nackledeep** — svirfneblin cartographer — evidence and geography.
4. **Rhava Coilscale** — bronze dragonborn weather engineer — science, environment, navigation.
5. **Naeva Two-Reeds** — human musician/translator — culture, language, memory.

Starting level target: **Level 1**. Pella/Rhava/Naeva are rewound to their beginnings for this five-book experiment rather than importing their later rough-draft levels.

The party should not instantly trust one another. They become a party because five different jobs all encounter the same impossible anomaly.

# BOOK I — ORIGIN
## Adventure: THE THING WITH NO HISTORY
**Levels:** 1–4  
**Cosmic age:** Origin  
**Question:** What existed before anything could be classified?

A black-violet object is found beneath a collapsed market cistern.

It has no detectable maker, age, magical school, mineral composition, religious mark, planar residue, or known function.

The party is assembled for mundane reasons:
- Ilyra secures the collapse.
- Brikket represents merchants whose property was damaged.
- Pella surveys the cavity.
- Rhava investigates strange pressure/weather changes underground.
- Naeva translates markings nearby—which turn out not to be writing.

### Five adventure movements
1. **The Collapse** — rescue workers before studying the artifact.
2. **The Empty Provenance** — investigate five mutually incompatible origin stories.
3. **The First Difference** — discover the object changes only when two observers disagree about it.
4. **The Unwitnessed Chamber** — dungeon where rooms acquire properties after being described.
5. **Leave It Unnamed** — climax asks whether categorizing the object is itself activating it.

### Monsters
Lantern Leeches, False Familiars, Griefcap Swarm, first manifestation of an Uncolored Echo.

### Advancement
L1 choose danger; L2 responsibility; L3 establish methods; L4 survive consequences.

### Party addition
**Siva-of-the-Open-Cap**, myconid memory tender, becomes recurring ally and optional sixth traveler.

### End state
The party does not learn what the object is. They learn that somebody else has been searching for objects like it.

**Target score:** 94+.

# BOOK II — DAWN
## Adventure: THE SECOND STAR ROAD
**Levels:** 5–8  
**Cosmic age:** Dawn  
**Question:** What happens when something notices the observer?

The party follows an ancient navigation custom: never travel toward the brightest star; follow the second.

It leads to fragments of the lost **People of the Second Star**, whose maps imply that early routes were designed not only to reach destinations but to avoid attention.

### Five adventure movements
1. **The Star That Listens** — navigation instructions begin appearing in dreams.
2. **Port of Two Skies** — living Spelljammer culture; trade, migration, families, ship labor.
3. **The Route-Eater** — return routes disappear behind travelers.
4. **The First Cartographer's Lie** — maps may describe where things are *allowed* to exist.
5. **Home Has Been Heard** — the Star Listener learns the party's home coordinates.

### Additions
**Nemea Brasswake**, bronze dragonborn navigator, joins as major companion/rival.
**Corren Quickstep**, halfling courier, becomes the party's interworld message runner.

### Monster progression
Star Listener, Route-Eater, Bell-Mimic colonies adapted to ship communication, alien navigation fauna.

### Party consequence
The party can save its own home by redirecting the listener toward another inhabited route. Doing so is easier and morally unacceptable to some members.

**Target score:** 95+.

# BOOK III — NOON
## Adventure: THE PERFECT MAP
**Levels:** 9–12  
**Cosmic age:** Noon  
**Question:** When does confidence become cruelty?

Civilization appears to have won.

A planar consortium has made travel astonishingly safe using Professor Caldris Vane's predictive mathematics. Trade flourishes. Families reunite. Fatal portal incidents collapse toward zero.

Then the party discovers the system's tiny error rate.

Travelers who contradict the model are quietly classified as anomalies and removed from the statistics.

### Five adventure movements
1. **The Safest City in the Multiverse** — let players enjoy the success before revealing its cost.
2. **0.03 Percent** — investigate people officially declared impossible.
3. **The Bright Archive** — encounter the shelf that cannot retain one classification.
4. **The Model Is Never Wrong** — Vane begins altering reality to match predictions.
5. **The Perfect Map Burns** — decide what knowledge must be preserved when dismantling an abusive system.

### Additions
**Kestra Vhal**, tiefling advocate, arrives to defend anomaly-classified travelers.
**Seris Moonquill**, aasimar astronomer, joins the scientific dispute.
Professor Vane begins as ally, becomes antagonist, and may survive as a future rival.

### Monsters
Eye-in-the-Map, Parallax Beholder, bureaucratic constructs, predictive duplicates.

### Party growth
The party is now influential enough that governments care what it says. This creates responsibility beyond dungeon victories.

**Target score:** 96+.

# BOOK IV — EVENING
## Adventure: THE KINDLY EYE
**Levels:** 13–16  
**Cosmic age:** Evening  
**Question:** How much freedom would people trade for certainty?

Reliable systems begin failing.

Not catastrophically. Slightly.

Constellations drift. Portals arrive minutes late. resurrection memories conflict. Maps disagree with streets. Divine messages cross recipients.

Then **The Kindly Eye** appears.

It answers every question correctly.

### Five adventure movements
1. **Four Minutes Late** — save people during a minor anomaly cascade.
2. **The Profession of Doubt** — work with constellation auditors, memory witnesses, portal insurers, temporal midwives, and professional skeptics.
3. **Ask Me Anything** — the Eye proves its answers are correct.
4. **The Evening Compact** — hostile civilizations cooperate because nobody can solve the crisis alone.
5. **The Last Question** — every party member is offered the answer to the thing they most desperately want to know.

### Additions
**Yara Zerth-Venn**, githzerai door theorist.
**Uu-Among-Us**, Far-Realm refugee.
**Morcant Vale**, dhampir mortuary physician.

The original five are no longer necessarily the strongest people present. Their importance comes from history, trust, and choices.

### Rival structure
Sszara Tlin, Kestra, Nemea, Morcant, and Yara crystallize into the **Ricochet Five**. Some are allies of the main party; some oppose its decisions.

### Monsters
Memory Leviathan, Oathstar Hunter, Silent Door Dead, escalating manifestations of the Kindly Eye.

### End state
Victory requires refusing perfect certainty while preserving institutions people still need.

**Target score:** 97+.

# BOOK V — NIGHT
## Adventure: THE LIGHT SOMEBODY MIGHT NEED
**Levels:** 17–20  
**Cosmic age:** Night  
**Question:** What remains meaningful when the universe promises nothing?

Stars vanish from navigational charts.

The Infinite Doors still function, but nobody knows whether the next opening will be the last.

Civilizations begin retreating inward.

The party receives no prophecy telling them they can win.

### Five adventure movements
1. **The Last Scheduled Ship** — decide who receives scarce transportation without pretending there is a perfect allocation.
2. **A Kitchen at the End of Everything** — Auntie Rell's crossroads becomes a refugee civilization.
3. **The Starless Navigator** — offers perfect centralized control of every surviving route.
4. **Five Rivals, One Key** — the Ricochet Five reunite; one possesses a control fragment.
5. **Leave the Lamp On** — finale is not about destroying Night. It is about creating enough trustworthy connections that people can keep living inside uncertainty.

### The Three Chosen connection
Alice, Solar, and Dhampir can appear here as independent high-level figures whose own stories intersect the crisis. They do **not** replace the party.

Alice brings the lesson that reality need not be completely understandable.
Solar brings redemption without cosmic bookkeeping.
Dhampir brings continuity of relationship beyond simplistic life/death categories.

Their presence makes the world feel interconnected while preserving the five-book party's own achievement.

### Final threats
Starless Navigator, evolved Kindly Eye consequences, route collapse, political panic, opportunistic powers, and one Origin anomaly that still cannot be explained at Level 20.

### Ending
The heroes cannot guarantee that every door will remain open.

They build a distributed network of communities capable of maintaining routes, sharing warnings, feeding travelers, preserving maps, and choosing cooperation without one cosmic ruler.

At Auntie Rell's kitchen, somebody asks why the lamp remains lit when the next travelers might not even possess eyes.

The answer remains:

**Because somebody else might need it.**

**Target score:** 98+.

# PARTY EXPANSION MODEL

Do not add companions merely because a new book starts.

A character joins when:
1. the party encounters a problem its current worldview cannot solve;
2. the newcomer has a culture, job, family, and goal independent of the heroes;
3. the newcomer disagrees meaningfully with at least one existing member;
4. joining costs them something;
5. they can leave again.

### Expected social orbit

**Book I:** 5 core + Siva ally.  
**Book II:** Nemea + Corren; first off-world contacts.  
**Book III:** Kestra + Seris + Vane relationship.  
**Book IV:** Yara + Uu + Morcant + Ricochet Five politics.  
**Book V:** large coalition of prior cultures, rivals, institutions, and Three Chosen intersections.

Not everyone travels in the active combat party. The growing cast becomes a **network**, preventing a ridiculous fifteen-person initiative order.
